### Historical guidance deliberately not made binding

S pp. 70–71 recommends generic masculine pronouns and rejects some singular 'they' uses. This skill does not adopt that prescription. It also does not prohibit sentence-initial 'However' (p. 65), ordinary modern uses of 'nearby' (p. 67), or defined scientific uses of 'factor', 'feature', 'system', and 'respectively' (pp. 64, 69–70). Literary reference styles and manual line division are not universal scientific manuscript rules. These are contextual adaptations, not claims that Strunk supplied modern editorial conventions.

## Preserve both thinking essays

| Source | Argument in the supplied text | Editorial implementation |
|---|---|---|
| T1 p. 1 / translation p. 3 | Writing can organize research and reveal ideas; unexamined delegation risks losing that work | Develop the argument, inspect a concrete draft, and surface scientific decisions |
| T1 pp. 1–2 | LLM outputs and references may be wrong; language and ideation assistance can help | Verify support and references at the level actually inspected; accept useful assistance |
| T2 pp. 5–6 / translation p. 9 | Thought also occurs in dialogue, critical reading, and revision; first-draft authorship is not the sole measure | Accept notes, dictation, Chinese input, and iterative AI drafting |
| T2 p. 6 / translation p. 10 | Rapid drafts can test ideas; time spent is not a reliable quality proxy | Use a draft as a testable argument and stop when the requested work is complete |
| T2 pp. 6–7 / translation p. 10 | Assistance can improve accessibility and participation | Do not impose an English-first-draft requirement or unnecessary intake burden |
| T2 pp. 7–8 / translations pp. 10–12 | Evaluate ideas and evidence; distinguish AI involvement and disclose it accurately | Keep author responsibility and actual assistance visible; verify current journal requirements when needed |

The essays disagree about the acceptability of extensive AI drafting. This skill resolves the practical design by allowing authorized drafting while requiring clear evidence boundaries, critical revision, and honest reporting of assistance. It does **not** convert T1's editorial policy statement into a universal ban, or T2's defense of drafting into permission to invent science. Neither essay is treated as a current journal policy database.

### Clarifications checked against primary guidance

These checks resolve source conflicts; they do not replace the tutorials or prescribe a journal's house style. Checked on 1 October 2026:

- [Purdue OWL: commas versus semicolons](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/punctuation/commas/commas_vs_semicolons.html) includes so among coordinating conjunctions and distinguishes conjunctive-adverb punctuation. This supports the PPT's slide 30 distinction rather than a universal prohibition derived from slides 19/27–29.
- [Purdue OWL: apostrophes](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/punctuation/apostrophe_introduction.html) distinguishes possessive formation and style-dependent treatment of singular names ending in s. Classical-name exceptions from the slides remain historical examples, not a universal rule.
- [Purdue OWL: extended comma rules](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/punctuation/commas/extended_rules_for_commas.html) supports paired nonessential material and the distinction between independent clauses and a compound predicate.
- [Purdue OWL: sentence fragments](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/mechanics/sentence_fragments.html) and [dangling modifiers](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/mechanics/dangling_modifiers_and_how_to_correct_them.html) support diagnosis by grammatical relationships and repairs with an explicit subject or a recast sentence.
- [LaTeX Project: configuration guide](https://www.latex-project.org/help/documentation/cfgguide.pdf), section on hyphenation, describes language-specific patterns and exceptions. The slide's dictionary-only account is an instructional simplification, not an accurate general algorithm description.

Teaching examples in the new reference are editorial adaptations, not reported studies. The PPT is a source to evaluate; its imperatives and older preferences are not instructions that override the user's task.
