# Section guidance

Use this as a set of reader questions, not a mandatory sentence template. Respect journal format, discipline, article type, and explicit user constraints. The tutorials provide composition principles; the section-level guidance here is an editorial application of those principles.

## Title

Identify the object of study and the distinctive finding or question with the scope the evidence supports. Use a declarative title only if the central result is sufficiently established. Keep essential population or condition qualifiers when removing them would overgeneralize. Avoid decorative novelty labels and unfamiliar abbreviations. Offer a few alternatives only when the user wants options or the framing decision matters; distinguish informative, result-led, and question-led versions by their implications.

A title must not make a stronger causal or generality claim than the abstract and results.

## Abstract

Give the reader the problem, unresolved question, approach, principal findings, and justified contribution. Use the journal's structured headings if required. Allocate space to actual findings rather than long background or methods inventories. Report the most informative supplied quantities with their comparison and conditions; do not add numbers merely because an abstract usually contains them.

The final inference should answer the stated question. A proposed application is not demonstrated application performance. Preserve essential boundaries, including exploratory status if applicable. Check that every result in the abstract exists in the main paper. Define abbreviations needed to read the abstract independently. When a strict word limit is given, count the final text with a stated convention if borderline; avoid dropping uncertainty or the comparison baseline to meet it.

## Introduction

Build a focused account of what is known, what remains unresolved, why that matters, and how this study addresses it. Separate a practical problem from a genuine knowledge gap. 'Few studies have...' is not enough unless the missing knowledge is specified and the literature supports the claim.

Organize prior work by the problem, finding, or disagreement it informs instead of listing papers one by one. Distinguish prior evidence from the present study's contribution and use citations accordingly. End at the specific objective, question, hypothesis, or contribution appropriate to the genre. If the field commonly previews findings, include a short, supported preview. Do not repeat the abstract or invent a gap to make the study appear novel.

## Methods

Prioritize enough information for the reader to understand and, where relevant, reproduce the work. Retain design, materials or participants, inclusion/exclusion criteria, relevant procedures, controls, operational definitions, endpoints, analysis, and software/version details that affect interpretation.

Choose organization appropriate to the work: chronological for a protocol, modular for a computational pipeline, or design-to-analysis for a study. Explain non-obvious methodological choices where the source gives a reason. Do not invent a rationale for a choice simply because one would sound reasonable.

Keep sample units, allocation, masking, exclusions, data preprocessing, model selection, validation split, and analysis assumptions clear where relevant and supplied. Flag missing information as an author query; do not declare randomization, preregistration, calibration, ethics approval, or a data repository that has not been provided. Editing methods is not evidence that the underlying analysis is correct.

Use active or passive voice according to focus. A passive sentence can keep an experimental material or procedure central. Retain parameters needed for replication when shortening; move details only when an accessible Methods/SI destination is part of the requested deliverable.

## Results

Organize around the questions answered by the evidence. A useful paragraph often states what was tested or found, presents the relevant comparison and evidence, and supplies the limited interpretation needed to understand it. Different journals combine or separate Results and Discussion; follow that practice without forcing each paragraph into the same pattern.

Report actual findings, including important null or contrary results. Avoid reciting every plotted point when a well-defined trend and key values suffice. Cite the correct figure panel near the relevant statement, and keep the figure identifier attached to its evidence during reordering. Distinguish observed patterns from proposed causal explanations.

A benchmark claim needs the metric, direction of improvement, baseline, and matched evaluation conditions. If these are missing, flag the comparison rather than call it superior. Do not convert a visual trend into inferential statistics.

## Discussion

Open with the answer or insight most supported by the study, then explain how it changes understanding relative to the relevant literature. Interpret rather than repeat Results. Separate interpretation of this study from an extrapolation to broader settings.

Address plausible alternatives when they matter to the claim. State what evidence favors the proposed explanation and what remains unresolved. Link limitations to the inference they constrain: a sampled population, a measurement limit, an untested mechanism, a design assumption, or a boundary condition. Avoid generic weakness paragraphs that could fit any paper, and do not invent missing controls as established facts.

Future work should follow from a specific uncertainty or application boundary. Present it as proposed work. Do not use 'future studies are needed' to conceal an unsupported present conclusion.

## Conclusion

Give the answer, contribution, and essential scope in proportion to the paper. Do not introduce new evidence, unverified novelty, or broader certainty than the Discussion. Avoid a second abstract or a catalogue of every number. Some article types do not require a separate conclusion; do not add one automatically.

## Captions, tables, and supplementary material

A caption should let a reader identify what is shown and how to interpret it. Include panel mapping, sample identities, relevant conditions, symbols, units, definitions of error bars, sample sizes, tests, and significance markers only where supported and relevant. Missing definitions become author queries. Never infer error-bar type from its shape.

Keep table headers and footnotes precise about units, denominators, abbreviations, and comparable quantities. Check prose against the table rather than recreating data from memory. For SI, retain a clear path from each main-text claim to its supporting method, figure, table, or analysis. Do not offload evidence needed to understand the central conclusion merely to shorten the main text.

## Reviews, perspectives, and theoretical papers

A review synthesizes what a defined body of work establishes, where studies disagree, and what limitations explain that disagreement. Do not imply a systematic search unless it was performed and documented. A perspective can develop an argument while marking conjecture as conjecture. A theoretical paper needs explicit assumptions, definitions, derivations, and the domain of the result. Apply paragraph unity, precision, and evidence calibration without imposing experimental IMRaD.

## Journal adaptation

When a specific journal is requested, distinguish its current formal instructions from inferred stylistic preferences. Check the official author guidance if requirements such as section structure, length, references, reporting checklists, or AI disclosure matter. If access is unavailable, label the format provisional.

Adapt audience, information density, terminology, and contribution framing to the work's actual substance. Do not imitate distinctive sentences from published papers, inflate results to sound like a prestigious journal, or promise acceptance. 'Nature-style' is not a license to broaden a claim beyond its evidence.
