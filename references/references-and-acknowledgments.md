# References, funding and acknowledgments

Use this guide when the user asks for reference formatting, an attribution check or acknowledgments. For verifying whether a publication supports a claim, use [argument and evidence](argument-and-evidence.md).

## Reconcile citations with the bibliography

Follow the journal's current style or its supplied template. Identify the citation system before editing. APA uses author and year, MLA commonly adds a page locator to the author, Chicago has notes and bibliography and author–date variants, and IEEE uses numbered citations. Field of study alone cannot determine the required style.

Preserve reference-manager fields, BibTeX keys, equation numbering, and meaningful locators. If the user supplies a Zotero, Mendeley or EndNote export, check imported metadata against the source, retain stable keys where possible, and render it in the requested style. For a cited-reference list, trace every marker to an entry and every entry back to its citation. Detect duplicate records for the same work, broken keys, mismatched author/year labels, and numbering changes after reordering. Retain separate preprint and version-of-record identities where the distinction matters. A generated bibliography still needs a read-back check.

## Give credit for the specific material used

Attach a citation to the proposition it supports. Use quotation marks and a locator for exact wording where appropriate, including definitions borrowed verbatim. Paraphrase from an understanding of the argument, then credit its source. If an author reuses text, a figure, data or analysis from their earlier publication, identify the overlap, cite the earlier work, and check the journal's directions for disclosure or permission. Widely shared background knowledge may need no citation. A precise empirical value, contested claim or source-specific interpretation generally needs traceable support.

## Draft acknowledgments from confirmed contributions

Gather the funder's official name and grant identifier, each contributor's name and specific help, and any facility, material or data provider. Record which details are confirmed and which require an author query. Match the journal's separate funding, authorship, author-contribution and acknowledgment fields. An acknowledgment cannot settle an authorship question.

Use a brief professional paragraph. Group support by its role when helpful. Name the contribution, for example assistance with instrument calibration or access to a dataset. For a journal manuscript, prioritize research support. A thesis can also acknowledge personal support if requested. Check permission to name people where relevant and keep only confirmed names, contributions, identifiers and permissions in clean prose. A missing grant number can be flagged outside the draft. Preserve official funder wording and the roles actually reported.
